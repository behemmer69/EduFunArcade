📐 Nyquist Studio: DSP Engineering Guide & Equations

Every digital recording balances two competing dimensions under a finite storage budget: Sampling Rate (fs) captures rapid changes in time (high frequencies), while Bits per Sample (N) captures subtle changes in amplitude (quiet details without quantization hiss). Below are the four core equations governing the studio—with your live knob settings plugged right in.
1. Nyquist–Shannon Sampling Theorem Time Axis
fNyquist = fs 2 ≥ fmax
Current fs = 12.0 kHz Max Clean Freq (fs/2) = 6.00 kHz

To capture a musical frequency fmax without distortion, your sampling rate fs must be at least twice that frequency. Any acoustic energy above fs/2 either folds back as harsh metallic aliasing tones (if unfiltered) or gets shaved off by the Anti-Aliasing Low-Pass Filter.

2. Quantization Levels & SQNR Amplitude Axis
L = 2N,   SQNRdB ≈ 6.02 · N + 1.76dB
Current N = 6.0 bits (64 steps) SQNR ≈ 37.9 dB

Each extra bit doubles the number of vertical stair-steps (L = 2N) and lowers the quantization noise floor by 6.02 dB. Tracks with wide dynamic range or soft decaying chords need higher N (8–12 bits), while loud, constant-volume synth cuts mask stair-step hiss even at 4–5 bits.

3. CD Bit Budget & Coupled Knobs Storage Constraint
Bused = T × fs × N ≤ Bmax ⇒ fs ≤ Bmax T · N
10s × 12.0 kHz × 6.0 bits = 720 kb Saved: 240 kb / 960 kb

For a T = 10 s track with a ceiling of Bmax = 960 kilobits, turning either knob down banks free space. Once Bused hits 960 kb, turning fs up mechanically forces N down along the hyperbola fs · N = 96 kbps.

4. Fidelity & Bit-Saver Bonus Formula Master Score
Stotal = Fbase × ( 1 + 0.75 · Bmax − Bused Bmax )
Base Fidelity: 0–1000 pts Current Bit-Saver Multiplier: ×1.19

Base Fidelity (Fbase) combines Spectral Integrity (500 pts) and Dynamic Cleanliness (500 pts). If Fbase ≥ 700 pts (Master Approved), every kilobit saved boosts your final score by up to +75%. Below 700 pts, the bit-saver bonus scales down so starving the track won't cheese the leaderboard.
